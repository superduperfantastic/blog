Valley of Fire State Park

May 6, 2018 ~ by tinna
Nevada ~ comments Valley of Fire State Park

Who else has this problem? Too many photos to process, so it takes awhile to even get to them. Don’t even get me started on videos. At this rate, I’ll be going through those in five more years. During this photo organization process, I came across these photos from our trip last April to Valley of Fire State Park. We were in Vegas for a friend’s wedding, but this ended up being one of the highlights of the trip.

Valley of Fire State Park is the oldest of Nevada’s state parks. From Vegas, we drove about an hour through what felt like the middle of nowhere. Then suddenly, the landscape opened up. It was spectacular.

Desert Flora

Covering approximately 35,000 acres, we barely scratched the surface with our short visit. We, of course, swung by the visitor center to figure out which hikes we could fit in our limited amount of time in the area. There were a few spots we could drive by, and then the rest of our time would be dedicated to hiking to the Fire Wave and then the White Domes Loop. Both were under 1.5 miles long, so perfect for this quick jaunt of ours!

Valley of Fire State Park - The Fire Wave

We enjoy easy picturesque hikes, and this was definitely one of them. The approximately 1.2 mile out-and-back trail is marked by intermittent arrows across sand and rock. It was very manageable for us as infrequent hikers.

On my way to the Fire Wave

The Fire Wave in all its glory - the angle did matter


Other random shots by the Fire Wave

Valley of Fire State Park - White Domes Loop

When it comes to hiking, we also really love loops. White Domes is not only a loop, but it’s an easy 1.2 mile trail with a lot to see. In that short distance, we descended into sand, passed an old movie set, hiked through a slot canyon, and saw a natural arch and multicolored sandstone. It packed a lot into just over a mile.

Shots along the White Domes Loop

It was warm during our visit last April. Even going such short distances with shade and a water bottle, we were quickly reminded that we could definitely have used more water on the trip. It’s the desert. You don’t want to mess around. We took breaks when we needed them - one of them was in the hole pictured above on the right. I was immediately drawn to climbing up into it because it was a purplish tint on the inside.

If I’m ever back in Vegas with a rental car, I’d absolutely head out to Valley of Fire again. We’re not usually the overnight backcountry camping types, but we could revisit these short hikes, do a longer hike or two, or maybe pick a different time of day to visit. The views in the early morning and early evening would probably yield the best display of colors!

Visit Valley of Fire State Park

It’s open 7 days a week, 365 days a year from sunrise to sunset. Entrance fee is $10. (as of 2017 trip)

Best times of year to visit: spring, fall, and winter.
