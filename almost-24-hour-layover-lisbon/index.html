My Almost 24-Hour Layover in Lisbon

Jul 7, 2026 ~ by tinna
Portugal ~ comments Lisbon, e-bike, layover

As mentioned in my life update from February (OOPS, how did I let so much time pass?), I went to Spain last November on a trip planned by El Lopo, a local bar here in San Francisco. They put together small group trips once or twice a year, and the stars finally aligned where I could join for the trip. My flight from San Francisco to Spain included a layover in Lisbon for about 21 hours. While I could have picked out a hotel by the airport to keep it easy, I figured, why not actually see Lisbon? Going directly into something active after a long flight also seemed like a better way to handle jet lag. Better than giving in to a nap that would likely throw off the rest of the trip, so that’s what I did! I landed before noon, and my connection didn’t leave until almost 9am the next morning.

To see Lisbon, I found an experience on Airbnb called Seven Hills of Lisbon by E-Bike. The tour ran from 2 to 4:30pm and at the time, came in at just under $30. Lisbon has a LOT of hills (it’s in the name), and doing this on an e-bike instead of on foot meant I actually had energy left to keep myself awake for the rest of the evening.

Upon landing, I took the metro from the airport, and then it was just a quick walk from the metro stop to the hotel. I stayed at the Convent Square Lisbon, an IHG property, which I booked through Chase Travel and happened to catch a $50 travel credit promo. Yay! They thankfully let me drop my bags off and do a quick little refresh before my room was available.

The meeting point for the tour was at Rua do Jardim do Tabaco, right along the water, but the shortest way there was through a mountain. It’s about a 20-25 minute walk up a hill and along some staircases, and luckily I arrived right on time. By the time I got there, I already had a pretty good preview of what “seven hills” was going to mean.

I don’t think I would have gotten to see this much of the city in one afternoon any other way. We started in Alfama at the old fish market, then climbed up to the Santo Estêvão viewpoint before heading to Castelo de São Jorge. From there we took a break at Miradouro da Graça, then rode up to Nossa Senhora do Monte, the highest viewpoint in the city.

Then we wound down through Mouraria, the neighborhood where Fado music got its start, before finishing up past the Lisbon Aqueduct and through Eduardo VII Park. After catching all these highlights, the tour ended where it began back at the shop.

After the tour wrapped up, I spent some time just walking around on my own trying to decide where to grab dinner before heading back to the hotel. It was a Sunday, so fewer restaurants were open than I expected. After doing some quick research online, I ended up booking a dinner reservation for O Frade. Unfortunately, all they had open was a 9pm spot for one.

Back at the hotel, I freshened up before dinner. One thing I missed out on due to the e-bike tour was the hotel’s daily tea ritual. It happens every day at 5pm in the hotel’s open-air cloister. A tea sommelier leads guests through blending their own tea from a selection of herbs and leaves. It ties back to Portugal’s history as the country that brought tea to Europe in the first place. It’s part of the hotel’s whole identity too, since the building used to be a 13th-century Dominican convent. I was off on the e-bike tour during that window, so I missed it. Worth knowing about if your schedule lines up better than mine did.

I grabbed a drink and a few snacks at the hotel’s restaurant and lounge to pass the time. Then I took the bus over to the restaurant, which was easy enough to figure out. I ended up arriving to my reservation a little early, but thankfully, they were able to seat me anyway. O Frade is known for their duck rice, so of course I had to order it. I also got a few other starters to go with it, which in hindsight was way too much food for one person. The duck rice alone was a substantial portion. But I powered through. I was not about to come all this way and not try the thing they’re known for. Worth it.

After dinner, transit options were more sparse that late, so I took a Bolt back to the hotel and went straight to bed. It was a fairly late night by that point, and I had an early flight to catch. I got to the airport on the earlier side the next morning, but it was still noticeably busy. Probably a mix of the usual Monday morning rush and people connecting onward to other European countries for the start of the work week.

Would I Do It Again?

Absolutely. A layover under 24 hours sounds like a throwaway travel day, but Lisbon proved that’s not always true. If you ever find yourself with a similar gap between flights, my advice is: leave the airport. Find one solid activity that covers a lot of ground, an e-bike tour is a great call for a hilly city. Pick a hotel close enough that you’re not wasting time getting back for your next flight.

Lisbon, I’ll be back. Next time for more than a day.

Have you made the most of a long layover? What other cities around the world are worth seeing if just for a day?
